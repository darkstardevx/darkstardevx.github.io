FIELD NOTE / FULL STATEMENT

The work is mine. The tools are many.

I’m a 52-year-old Gen X builder who grew up in the 80s and 90s. Before 1995, I mostly just liked taking things apart to see what was inside. My dad encouraged that curiosity. I hated computers in high school—I was into thrash metal, playing guitar, and music is still my number-one love, with computers right beside it.

Then came 1995: AOL, CompuServe, Mosaic, Netscape, and the first real pull toward code. Anything with a case screwed shut or a “view source” option caught my attention. That instinct became a career: web design, network installs, custom PCs, Android ROMs, Node.js, and now Rust, Lua, Quickshell/QML, and the Cybercore ecosystem.

For more than 30 years I worked as an automotive technician—electrical diagnostics, LAN/CAN bus systems, ECM reprogramming, engine builds, and nearly every system in a car. That work taught me the rule I carry into software: the tool in your hand doesn’t make the diagnosis. You do.

Everything on this site—and everything it links to—is architected, styled, and reviewed by hand. AI assistance handles grunt work, not decisions. Working smart isn’t a shortcut—it’s the point. The judgment, constraints, final call, and responsibility for what ships are still mine.

— darkstardevx
